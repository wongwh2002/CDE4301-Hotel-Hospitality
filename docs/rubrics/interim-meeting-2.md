## Summary

The presentation feels too dense and descriptive, and its story does not clearly connect the hotel problem to the prototype. Rebuild it around a concise, evidence-backed case: why service quality and personalization matter, where guest information is lost or unavailable to staff, what the prototype does, and what testing has shown. Keep the prototype and its evidence central. Do not claim that personalization or the prototype has already improved retention or revenue without evidence.

## Main feedback

- Introduce the hotel context and relevant outcomes—repeat guests/retention, occupancy, average daily rate, and service quality—then connect them to guest needs with sourced evidence.
- Synthesize research across hotels and use stakeholder evidence to support a specific problem: useful guest information may be captured but not passed to or retrieved by the next relevant staff member during a stay.
- Use a few relevant LQA/Forbes standards to ground the lounge/F&B scenario. Explain what they measure and how they inform requirements; do not list many standards or treat a score as proof of business impact.
- Agree on a consistent prototype breakdown. A candidate framing from the discussion is guest recognition, interaction capture/processing, and context retrieval/display. Link each capability to a requirement and a test.
- Cut repetitive tables, long comparisons, and detailed future architecture from the main story. Show a working demo and distinguish implemented, simulated, and planned parts.
- Present experiments as question → method → measured result → decision. Verify whether reported performance percentages were measured or only estimated.
- Separate technical measures, such as recognition quality and latency, from user/service outcomes, such as cue comprehension and perceived personalization. Treat retention and revenue as longer-term hypotheses.
