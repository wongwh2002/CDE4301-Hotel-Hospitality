# Hotel frontline task priorities and personalisation

- **Research date:** 2026-10-21
- **Purpose:** Assess whether hotel frontline employees prioritize time-bound operational work, such as arrivals and check-in, over individualized service and use of guest context.

## Short assessment

The evidence supports a cautious claim that hotel employees face competing service and operational demands, and that workload pressure can make service performance harder to sustain. The available sources do **not** establish an industry-wide ranking in which check-in or other transactions consistently take priority over personalisation. The strongest direct local example is from the Novotel visit notes: staff reportedly may not have time to check guest profiles during food-and-beverage service, while check-in is described as having a three-minute brand standard. These are observations from one property and do not prove that the same workflow or priority order applies at PARKROYAL.

For the report, frame the proposed link as a hypothesis: **time-bound operational work may leave staff less time to retrieve and act on guest context during service.** Call this “competing demands,” “workload pressure,” or “time pressure” unless the project directly measures cognitive load.

## Evidence

### Hotel employee study: service priority under workload pressure

Vashdi, Katz–Navon, and Delegach conducted an observational, multilevel study of 245 employees in 39 departments across 13 hotels in one luxury chain in Israel, pairing employee surveys with manager ratings. They examined whether employees perceived service as a priority relative to competing goals, employees’ emotional-labour strategies, workload pressure, and service performance. The indirect relationship from department-level service priority through deep-acting emotional labour to manager-rated service performance was present at low workload pressure, but not at medium or high pressure. Workload pressure by itself was not associated with deep acting. [Publisher article](https://doi.org/10.1177/19389655211029912) · [University of Haifa research record](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/service-priority-climate-and-service-performance-among-hospitalit/)

This is direct hotel-employee evidence that workload pressure can condition a relationship between service priorities and service performance. Because the study is observational, it does not show that workload causes poorer performance. It does **not** compare check-in with personalisation, measure access to guest profiles, or show a specific personalisation failure. Its sample also does not establish the same effect at Singapore hotels or PARKROYAL.

### Novotel visit: local example of profile use competing with service work

The Novotel visit notes record a three-minute brand standard for check-in and report that queues can occur during peak arrival periods. The notes also report that food-and-beverage employees may not have time to consult ACDC during service; staff may rely on memory and a physical VIP board, and preference information may not transfer consistently between shifts. These are interview and visit notes, not a time-and-motion study or a measured comparison of task priority. [Novotel visit notes](../../hotel-visit/novotel-formatted.md#check-in-process) · [Personalisation notes](../../hotel-visit/novotel-formatted.md#personalisation-in-food-and-beverage)

This is the clearest local support for investigating the proposed mechanism: transaction and service demands are visible in the workflow, while checking the guest profile can be deferred. It is evidence about the Novotel account only; ACDC should not be attributed to PARKROYAL without confirmation.

### PARKROYAL visit: relevant setting, but not a priority comparison

The PARKROYAL notes describe manual guest routing at checkout, luggage work that adds to bell-service workload, and stakeholder interest in smart glasses for VIP recognition in the club lounge. They identify possible display fields such as name, allergies, and preferences, alongside privacy and access-control concerns. They do not report that lounge staff rank check-in or another named task above personalisation, nor do they establish that the glasses improve service. [PARKROYAL visit notes](../../hotel-visit/parkroyal-main-points.md)

## Report framing

Prefer:

> Hotel employees balance time-sensitive operational work with guest-facing service. During busy periods, staff may have less opportunity to retrieve and use recorded guest context; this project will investigate that gap in a PARKROYAL club-lounge workflow.

Avoid claiming that staff do not value personalisation, that check-in is universally prioritized over it, or that high cognitive load has already been demonstrated. The project’s current notes define personalisation as staff using relevant guest context to adapt service, and distinguish recognition, context access, service adaptation, and the guest’s perceived fit. A face or name match alone is therefore not evidence that personalisation occurred. [Project definition](defining-hotel-service-personalization.md)

The POC can test a bounded version of the hypothesis: does a concise, authorized cue help staff retrieve and act on a relevant lounge detail during the service moment? A useful evaluation should distinguish task pressure, profile retrieval, staff action, guest-perceived relevance, interruption, and recognition errors. The POC should not claim reduced workload or better guest outcomes unless those outcomes are measured.

## Highest-value evidence to collect at PARKROYAL

- Ask lounge employees to walk through one recent busy service period and identify which tasks were time-bound, interruptible, or deferrable.
- Observe how staff currently learn that a guest has arrived, access permitted guest context, and decide whether to use it.
- Record profile-retrieval time, whether the information changes the service action, and whether staff find the cue useful or distracting.
- Separate any identity-match result from the service action and guest response; a correct match does not itself demonstrate personalisation.
- Confirm which profile fields are actually available and authorized for lounge employees before selecting examples such as allergies, food preferences, meeting plans, or prior conversation.
