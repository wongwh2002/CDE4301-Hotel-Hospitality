# Hotel meeting and conference profitability

**Research date:** 2026-10-10
**Purpose:** Assess whether hotel meetings and conferences are a meaningful profit stream and how this differs from VIP guest service.

## Short assessmen

Meetings and conferences can contribute several revenue streams to a hotel: room blocks, meeting-room rental, banquet and catering, audiovisual services, and service charges. The economics depend on the property, space utilization, event mix, food and labor costs, and whether the group fills rooms that would otherwise go unsold.

Recent CBRE U.S. data show hotel F&B can contribute to operating profit, but the latest results are mixed for convention hotels: first-half 2025 banquet revenue fell at convention hotels even as it rose at resorts. Older Canadian benchmarking found higher overall F&B department margins at hotels with more meeting space, while cautioning that meeting capacity must fit the property and local market. These data make conference business a plausible hotel profit area, not a guarantee of high margins.

The project opportunity would also differ from VIP recognition. It would likely center on giving event, banquet, and AV staff the event organizer’s requirements in time—such as room setup, schedule, dietary or accessibility needs, and service changes. That is a **working hypothesis** about a possible workflow, not a finding from the hotel visits. It would need its own validation with event staff and organizers.

## Evidence on revenue and profi

### Group business is a meaningful part of full-service hotel demand

CBRE’s 2025 U.S. Trends sample lists full-service hotel market mix as **70.3% transient, 23.5% group, and 6.2% contract**. This shows group demand is a material segment for full-service hotels; it does not mean 23.5% of profit comes from meetings, and the figure should not be generalized to a Singapore property. [CBRE, *Trends in the Hotel Industry: 2025 U.S. Sample Report*](https://pip.cbrehotels.com/docs/default-source/samples/trends/annual-trends/2025-annual-us-trends-sample.pdf?sfvrsn=2)

### Events contribute through both meeting services and F&B

CBRE analyzed **2,669 U.S. full-service, resort, and convention hotels** with 2023–2024 operating statements, plus monthly data from **866 properties** for January–June 2025. In the first half of 2025, F&B revenue per occupied room rose **3.8%**, while total hotel revenue rose **3.0%**. The average F&B department profit margin rose from **28.7% to 29.1%**. Those are aggregate F&B results, not a margin for conferences alone.

The same analysis found banquet revenue at convention hotels declined **7.3%** in the first half of 2025, while it grew **8.7%** at resort hotels. CBRE also reported growth across the sample in public-room rental (**9.5%**) and service-charge revenue (**7.9%**). This mix shows why a conference business case should include the whole event package while checking local demand and costs. [CBRE, “Hotel Food and Beverage – A Bright Spot in 2025”](https://www.cbre.com.sg/insights/articles/hotel-food-and-beverage-a-bright-spot-in-2025)

CBRE’s earlier analysis of **2,500 U.S. full-service, resort, and convention hotels** found that public-room rental and audiovisual revenue increased as conventions and meetings began recovering in 2022. This supports those as event-related revenue lines, but the comparison occurred during post-pandemic recovery and is not a current forecast. [CBRE, “Hotel Financial Performance Influenced by Changes in Hotel Food and Beverage Offerings”](https://www.cbre.com/insights/briefs/hotel-financial-performance-influenced-by-changes-in-hotel-food-and-beverage-offerings)

### Meeting space can improve F&B economics, but the evidence is older and contextual

HVS reviewed more than **200 Canadian hotel operating statements** and segmented F&B results by meeting-space capacity. In its May 2013 report, hotels with at least **20,000 square feet** of meeting space had an average F&B departmental profit margin of **25.7%**; those with less than **2,500 square feet** averaged **7.5%**. HVS attributed the stronger results partly to banquet volume, more efficient menu planning and labor use, and lower food costs. It also warned that meeting space must match hotel size and surrounding demand. The figures are old, Canadian, and describe total F&B department margins by meeting-space category—not the margin of a conference or banquet by itself. [HVS, “Benchmarking Canadian Hotel F&B Profitability”](https://www.hotelnewsresource.com/pdf13/HVS0705131.pdf)

## How to evaluate a conference opportunity

Estimate contribution for the **whole group contract**, not room rental alone:

> Event contribution = room-block contribution + meeting-room rental + catering/banquet contribution + A/V and service-charge contribution − event labor − food and beverage cost − setup, cleaning, equipment, and commission costs − opportunity cost of rooms or space displaced.

This is a management measurement framework, not a published industry formula. Compare contribution against a realistic alternative use of the rooms and space. A group can produce substantial revenue yet contribute little profit if it requires costly customization, extra staffing, discounted rooms, or displaces higher-value business.

## Implication for the projec

This could be a more commercially concrete direction if the target hotel relies on meetings or group business. The first research question should be operational: **Can event and banquet staff reliably access the organizer’s current requirements and changes during setup and service?** Interview event sales/planning, banquet, and AV staff, and ask them to walk through recent events where information was late, incomplete, or changed. Then measure rework, staff time, service failures, event feedback, repeat bookings, and event contribution.

This is a different problem from helping lounge staff personalize an individual guest’s service. It may involve organizers and corporate accounts as the repeat customer, with event attendees as service recipients. Confirm which person and workflow matter before choosing a solution such as smart glasses.

## Evidence limits

- Most quantitative evidence here is U.S. or Canadian; it is not a direct forecast for Singapore.
- Hotel group demand is broader than conference demand and can include social events and other group room blocks.
- Departmental F&B margin is not the net profit of an event after all property costs or the displaced value of the space.
- The HVS meeting-space comparison is from 2013; the recent CBRE conference-specific result is a revenue change, not event profit margin.
- None of these sources establish that guest-data personalization increases conference bookings or profit.
