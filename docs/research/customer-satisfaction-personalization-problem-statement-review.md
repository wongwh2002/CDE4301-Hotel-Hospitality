| The 2010 Griffith study shows satisfaction is a main driver of retention. | The cited paper is Wilkins (2010), an importance-performance analysis of 664 usable responses from eight Queensland four- and five-star hotels. ## Medallia methodology and measurement

The Medallia headline combines results from two separate U.S.-weighted panel surveys; the **61%** and **25%** figures do not come from one sample:

- **Hotel and retail personalization survey (November 2023):** 1,749 hotel guests and 1,905 retail consumers were asked about a recent stay or transaction. Medallia says it used Medallia Agile Research and its Sense360 research panel, with weighting based on U.S. Census age and gender distributions. The published method does not report a response rate, probability-sampling procedure, or margin of error. [Press release and methodology](https://www.medallia.com/press-release/medallia-research-finds-61-percent-of-consumers-are-willing-to-spend-more-for-personalized-experiences/) · [Full report](https://www.medallia.com/wp-content/uploads/pdf/resources/Medallia-Understanding-Personalization-Efforts-in-the-Hospitality-and-Retail-Industries-Report.pdf)
- **“Highly personalized”:** Respondents rated how personalized their recent stay or interaction felt from 0 to 10. The question told them to use their own judgment about what “personalized” means; **9–10** counted as high, **7–8** moderate, and **0–6** low. This is perceived personalization, not an objective audit against a defined service checklist. The hotel-only result is **23%** rating 9–10; the retail result is **26%**. The release's 25% headline summarizes the two sectors. [Full report, pp. 2–3](https://www.medallia.com/wp-content/uploads/pdf/resources/Medallia-Understanding-Personalization-Efforts-in-the-Hospitality-and-Retail-Industries-Report.pdf)
- **During-stay 46%:** This is a different measure. Guests were shown listed personalization elements for each stage and asked which they noticed or experienced. The 46% is the average recall of listed elements during the hotel stay, not the percentage of guests rating the overall stay highly personalized.
- **61% willing to spend more (August 2023):** A separate survey of 2,000 U.S. consumers ran August 9–11 through Medallia's CheckMarket platform and Sense360 panel, weighted by U.S. Census age and gender distributions. Respondents rated agreement on a five-point scale with a general statement about willingness to spend more if a company customized its service; 61% selected agree or strongly agree. This is hypothetical, self-reported willingness across companies, not hotel-specific or observed spending. The report says *n*=2,000 in its method but labels the chart *n*=2,001. [Consumer report and method](https://www.medallia.com/wp-content/uploads/pdf/resources/Medallia-Report-How-Consumers-Really-Feel-About-Personalization.pdf)

## Defensible wording

> Research suggests that travelers value recognition and relevant service, while U.S. hotel guests in one survey recalled fewer personalization elements during the stay than at other stages. This supports investigating how guest context is used during in-person service, but does not show that the same gap exists at PARKROYAL or that it causes lower retention. We will map the lounge workflow and test whether relevant, current guest context reaches staff in time to improve the service interaction.

Keep satisfaction and retention as possible downstream outcomes. The first problem to validate is the local service gap: what useful context exists, whether staff can access it at the right moment, whether it changes the service, and whether guests find that change relevant and comfortable. Smart glasses and face matching remain solution hypotheses. This follows the project's [current working context](../agents/hotel-smart-glasses-poc.md); a broader draft problem statement and discovery plan are in the linked problem-framing document above.
