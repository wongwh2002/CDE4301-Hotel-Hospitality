# Interim slides: service standards and competitor capabilities

Checked 2026-11-02. This note separates publicly documented features from inferred limitations and does not establish which features a particular hotel has configured.

## Luxury service standards

**Public category-level mapping.** LQA publicly describes `Food Quality` (up to 54 standards) as culinary excellence, presentation, and adherence to menu descriptions; `Emotional Intelligence` (up to 272) as personalizing interactions and displaying empathy; and `Efficiency` (up to 151) as attentive and efficient service. These categories support the project rationale, but are not exact numbered audit points. [LQA hotel assessments](https://lqagroup.com/hotel-assessments/).

FTG's public 2021 hotel overview exposes sections such as `Hotel Dining` and `Bar/Lounge Service`, with classifications including `Food & Beverage Quality`, `Efficiency`, and `Graciousness, Thoughtfulness & Sense of Personalized Service`. These are specific section/classification matches for the slide, **not** a claim about an individual numbered criterion. FTG says the 2025/26 standards are supplied by request and refined annually. FTG describes its anonymous inspections as evaluating up to 900 objective criteria and emphasizes customized service, but the reviewed first-party pages do **not** establish a current numbered standard for allergy handling, preference recall, or service-recovery follow-up. [FTG 2021 overview](https://secure.s.forbestravelguide.com/img/partner-services/DownloadStandardsOverview.pdf) · [FTG About](https://registration.forbestravelguide.com/about) · [FTG 2025/26 standards access](https://summit.forbestravelguide.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Official-Brand_Forbes-Travel-Guide.pdf).

FTG's public description of Hong Kong restaurant Noi reports that the restaurant accommodates allergies. It is an example of a property's practice, not a published universal FTG criterion. [FTG Noi inspector highlights](https://www.forbestravelguide.com/restaurants/hong-kong-china/noi).

**Leading Quality Assurance (LQA).** Mandarin Oriental's own report identifies LQA as an international service-quality benchmarking process used by the hotel group. It does not identify numbered allergy, preference, or recovery-follow-up clauses. [Mandarin Oriental 2019 sustainability report](https://photos.mandarinoriental.com/is/content/MandarinOriental/_Corporate/PDFs/mandarin-oriental-sustainability-report-2019-.pdf).

A public third-party upload labelled “In Room Dining Leading Quality Assurance Standards 2023” lists items 4 and 6 about menu/ingredient/allergy questions and acknowledging dietary requirements disclosed during the stay. It is not an owner-published copy, may be hotel-specific or outdated, and does not establish that these numbers apply to a lounge or the current edition. Treat it as a lead for partner verification, **not** as a confirmed criterion. [Third-party upload](https://anyflip.com/itxup/azhx/basic/). No current owner-published numbered clause was located for allergy handling, preference recall, or recovery follow-up. The team's “automatic audit failure” and “No Repetition” statements also remain unverified. Obtain an authorized, dated LQA and FTG edition specifying the hotel, department, and exact clause before citing item numbers or an automatic-failure consequence.

## Wearables and developer support

**Solos AirGo V series.** Solos now publicly advertises an iOS/Android SDK for AirGo V1/V2 camera models and AirGo 3/A5 audio models. It lists V1 photo streaming/capture, V2 video/Wi-Fi additions and sample mobile apps, with access via a paid SDK kit. This contradicts a blanket claim that Solos provides no custom development path. The page does not establish whether the desired model can run on the glasses, whether a particular display exists, or whether the SDK supports this team's identity use case; verify exact SKU, permissions, processing location and terms. [Solos developer page](https://solosglasses.com/pages/developers).

**Meta AI glasses.** Classify the built-in Meta AI experience as an out-of-box consumer assistant, not a hotel workflow. Meta's current developer documentation describes two paths: Device Access Toolkit (DAT) extends iOS/Android apps with supported camera, audio, motion and compatible display capabilities; standalone Web Apps run only on Meta Ray-Ban Display. Features vary by model and supported country. Meta's FAQ says integrations can process data locally or via cloud/edge, with the Meta AI app required for pairing. The public pages document supported device interfaces, but do not describe arbitrary custom-model deployment into the native glasses AI/system layer. A hotel-specific app plus service/backend or approved PMS integration is needed for guest matching, OPERA profile access, preference handling, permissions and cross-shift handoff. Describe Meta as a proprietary, API-controlled platform rather than simply saying “closed source”: the public DAT GitHub repo points to Meta Wearables Developer Terms, and the SDK is distributed through its official repos. The “no hardware processing” claim is too broad because DAT exposes glasses camera/audio/motion/display functions to apps; the narrower claim is that direct, arbitrary on-glasses model deployment is not documented. Verify exact device, market, developer terms, and where the app runs. [Meta DAT](https://developers.meta.com/wearables/device-access-toolkit/) · [Meta FAQ](https://developers.meta.com/wearables/faq/) · [Meta Web Apps](https://developers.meta.com/wearables/web-apps/) · [Meta Ray-Ban product page](https://www.ray-ban.com/usa/ray-ban-meta-ai-glasses?showAIComparator=true) · [DAT repository terms](https://github.com/meta/meta-wearables-dat-android/blob/main/LICENSE).

**Vuzix.** Vuzix documents standalone Android applications on the M400/M4000 and standard Android camera APIs; other devices such as Z100 use phone-side application processing. This rules out a blanket claim that the range does not allow custom apps. It does not guarantee a specific ML model's compatibility, speed, battery life or hardware acceleration. Test the model on the chosen device. The M400 lacks Google Play services; Vuzix also says routine OS security updates are no longer planned and support is planned through December 2028. [Developer resources](https://support.vuzix.com/docs/developer-resources) · [M400 application development](https://support.vuzix.com/docs/overview-10) · [Camera API](https://support.vuzix.com/docs/m400-m4000-technical-details) · [Google Play support](https://support.vuzix.com/docs/do-the-m-series-smart-glasses-support-the-google-play-store) · [M400 product support](https://www.vuzix.com/products/m400-smart-glasses).

**Presentation wording:** distinguish proprietary operating systems or SDK terms from the ability to run a custom application and from where an AI model runs. hotelkit Open API, Oracle OHIP, Meta DAT, Solos SDK, and Vuzix Android support integration or custom development paths. “Closed source, so custom models cannot run” is not supported across these products. Compare a named model and actual system architecture, with documented limits rather than treating a brand as a single platform.
