- Engineering
- Safety

### Food and beverage

- Kitchen
- Service

- Food Exchange
- Bar

- Dedicated food-and-beverage employees

### Staffing arrangements

- The workforce includes full-time and part-time employees.
- Part-time staffing is used for events.
- Housekeeping is outsourced.
- When part-time hours need to be reduced, full-time employees may be rotated.
- Manpower remains a major difficulty across hotel operations.
- The manager stated that the hotel would like approximately 50% more manpower.
- Local workers are reportedly less willing to work in hotel service roles.
- Foreign-worker quotas limit recruitment.
- Employees come from different backgrounds and speak different languages, which can make training and communication more difficult.
- Nationalities mentioned included employees from China, Indonesia, India, Myanmar and Bhutan.

## Front Office Operations

### Shift coverage and manpower

- The front office operates with a lean team and overlapping shifts.
- The notes mention approximately ten reception employees serving two hotels across three shifts.
- One employee may cover the morning shift, depending on the roster.
- Staffing needs vary according to occupancy, departures, arrivals and time of day.
- Peak arrival periods can produce queues and longer waiting times.
- A queue was observed during the visit.

### Check-in process

- The brand standard is to complete check-in within three minutes.
- Check-in may involve:
  - Identifying loyalty status
  - Processing or confirming payment
  - Explaining loyalty benefits or entitlements, such as a drink benefit
- Early-arriving guests may not know the official check-in time.
- This was particularly associated in the discussion with some Indian and Korean travellers arriving early in the morning.
- Early check-in is chargeable, but staff may find it difficult to make guests wait or impose the charge consistently.
- Staff may waive the charge for guests with elderly family members or children.
- Some guests pre-book the previous night to guarantee an early-morning room.

### Language coverage

- The manager considered it useful for every shift to include multilingual employees.
- Multilingual coverage has become harder to maintain.
- The notes mention two Chinese-speaking employees and employees who speak English or Indian languages.
- Google Translate may be used when no employee can communicate directly with a guest.
- Language capability affects scheduling.

### Individual and group arrivals

- Individual arrivals require passports to be processed one at a time.
- For large groups, guest and passport details may be provided in advance.
- Reservations and room keys can then be prepared before the group arrives.
- Keys may be handed to the tour leader according to the group’s arrival time.
- A large business group example involved an Indian pharmaceutical company booking an event and approximately 500 rooms.

## Housekeeping and Room Readiness

### Cleaning workflow

- A supervisor coordinates the housekeeping workflow.
- A linens may first be stripped by supervisor (takes the longest time) before a room attendant cleans it.
- Room cleaning includes making the bed, which was described as one of the longest tasks.
- Checked-out rooms require full cleaning.
- Occupied rooms are serviced according to a different standard operating procedure.
- Guest preferences may affect where items are placed in an occupied room.
- Lost-and-found items and unusual guest behaviour must be reported according to safety and security procedures.

### Room-status workflow

- The notes use the following room-status codes:
  - `VD`: checked-out room [exact expansion not confirmed]
  - `VC`: housekeeping/room-attendant stage [exact expansion not confirmed]
  - `VCI`: room inspected and ready [exact expansion not confirmed]
- The front office can release a room only after it reaches the inspected status.
- The notes mention only ten housekeeping supervisors.
- A shared system or application shows whether a guest has checked out and whether a room is ready.

### Workload and quotas

- Room attendants work approximately 9 a.m. to 9 p.m. using room quotas.
- Work beyond the quota may be treated as overtime or paid per room.
- A fixed number of rooms is assigned daily.
- A room displaying “do not clean” may still count within the assigned quota.
- Cleaning time varies according to:
  - Guest profile
  - Family occupancy
  - Room condition
  - Bed configuration, such as twin beds versus a queen bed
- Sick leave and excessive room assignments can make it difficult for the team to cope.
- A room attendant may be reassigned as a runner when manpower is insufficient.
- Housepersons or runners deliver items requested by guests.
- Linen and delivery work on each floor can be physically heavy.
- Room attendants are generally younger, while an elderly employee mentioned in the notes performs sewing or houseperson duties.

### Delayed room release

- Housekeeping may be unable to keep pace with the volume of arriving guests.
- Guests may wait approximately 30–60 minutes for rooms to be released.
- Delays can arise when rooms have not been cleaned or inspected before arrivals.
- Peak periods, public holidays and major events increase the workload.
- National Day was mentioned as a period when Marina Bay Sands-related demand increases occupancy and makes cleaning more difficult.

### Room changes and repeated work

- Novotel rooms are generally the same size (in stevens)
- Guests may be unhappy with:
  - Room size
  - Assigned floor
  - View
  - Missing or unsuitable amenities
  - Safety or hygiene concerns
- A different view or floor is subject to availability.
- Room-change requests occur frequently, potentially during every shift.
- When a guest changes rooms, the original room may need to be tidied or cleaned again.

## Communication and Coordination

- Different departments conduct separate briefings.
- WhatsApp group chats are currently used for cross-department communication.
- Someone must monitor the chats and ensure that each matter is addressed.
- Front office and housekeeping communicate through group chats about room status and guest needs.
- Information may not transfer consistently between shifts.
- Within food and beverage, staff may tell colleagues on the same shift that a guest has already arrived, but there may be no consistent handover between shifts.
- Guest feedback is circulated manually to department leaders.

## Guest Information and Access

- Front-of-house employees need broad guest information to serve guests effectively.
- Not every employee should have access to all guest information.
- Guest data may include:
  - Preferences
  - Favourite fruit
  - Allergies
  - Previous stays
  - Negative or concerning guest behaviour
- The discussion highlighted a tension between giving staff useful information and protecting guest information.

## Guest Expectations and Conflict

- Guests sometimes complain about matters that are already disclosed on the hotel website, such as room size.
- Some guests may not read or understand information about room size, check-in time or charges.
- Staff may face conflict when enforcing hotel rules or explaining limitations.
- Some guests are described as highly particular about rooms, amenities, floors and views.
- Staff discretion may result in exceptions for families, children or elderly guests.

### Missing hotel property and repeated requests

- Guests sometimes remove items from rooms.
- Cutlery, forks, spoons and ice buckets were mentioned as items that may be lost or not returned.
- The hotel may charge for certain utensils or service items.
- Multiple requests to deliver an ice bucket or other items can reduce staff productivity.
- The notes suggest that the hotel prefers guests to dine in the restaurant rather than repeatedly request utensils for food consumed in rooms.

### Payment and extended stays

- Payment arrangements include prepaid bookings, payment on arrival, flexible rates and non-refundable rates.
- Billing may use cash, credit cards or virtual credit cards.
- Cash-paying guests may create financial exposure if payment is not secured.
- Some guests extend their stays day by day and pay in cash (suspected vice activity).
- Billing instructions are entered into the hotel system.

## Guest Personalisation and Loyalty

### Accor Customer Digital Card

- The Accor Customer Digital Card (ACDC) is a global guest-information system.
- It records guest preferences and interests.
- The hotel and guest can both update information.
- The system includes a satisfaction tab.
- Guest profiles may be reviewed approximately three days before arrival.
- Platinum-tier and higher members receive particular attention before arrival.
- Silver-tier and higher members may receive priority.
- Approximately 15% of arrivals were described as members with usable preference information.
- Approximately 20–30% may be new members. [Exact meaning and denominator not confirmed.]
- Before ACDC, matching guests could be difficult when people had the same name or nationality.
- Preference information should be accurate so employees do not repeatedly ask the same questions.

### Collecting and using preferences

- A guest-experience manager may review upcoming arrivals and room-setup preferences.
- Employees may speak to guests to learn their needs.
- First-time members may state what they want or need in the room.
- Preferences mentioned include:
  - Fruit
  - Allergies
  - Coffee
  - Sweetener
  - Turndown preferences
  - Sleeping position or which side of the bed is used
- Consistency is important when applying recorded preferences.
- Higher-tier loyalty members were described as more likely to be highly satisfied.
- Repeat visits and a lack of complaints were mentioned as signs that personalisation may be helping.

### Personalisation in food and beverage

- Food-and-beverage employees often recognise repeat guests from memory.
- Employees may not have time to check ACDC during service.
- A physical VIP wall may show guest preferences, such as the way a guest prefers eggs at breakfast.
- Preference information may not transfer consistently between shifts.

- Vacant rooms represent inventory that cannot be recovered after the night passes.

- A room controller assigns rooms daily while considering requests, subject to availability.

## Technology and Hotel Systems

### Reservation and property-management systems

- A web-based Accor reservation system shows room types and rates.
- The revenue team adjusts rates according to forecasts and trends.
- OPERA Cloud is used as the property-management system.
- It displays and updates bookings.
- The system distinguishes first-time and repeat guests and includes past and future stay dates.
- The room controller uses booking information when assigning rooms.
- Systems are now described as relatively well integrated.
- The systems are server- and cloud-based.

### Guest requests and calls

- Guests can use QR codes in rooms to request services.
- One operator may handle telephone calls for all 772 rooms, including external calls.
- Employees use duty phones and an application, including part-time employees.
- Requests can arrive through the application or face-to-face.
- Standard operating procedures guide call handling.
- Requests may take approximately 15–30 minutes, depending on the request.
- Calls cannot easily be outsourced because an external operator may not know property-specific details.

### Systems and projects mentioned

- Hub OS software is used or planned for operational coordination.
- A self-check-in kiosk was pending at the time of the conversation.
- A delivery robot for in-room dining was mentioned.
- The manager expressed concern that excessive bot use could reduce customer satisfaction because guests still value human interaction.
- A comparison with highly automated service in Japan was mentioned.

## Training, Job Design and Service Consistency

### Training and cross-functional duties

- The first three months of employment are used for training.
- New employees are expected to learn broadly across hotel operations.
- Employment duties may include other tasks assigned by supervisors.
- Morning briefings are used to introduce new practices.
- More briefing time is needed when employees come from varied environments and language backgrounds.
- The hotel encourages employees to move between areas and learn different work.
- Experience operating as a government quarantine facility during COVID-19 demonstrated the need for staff to respond flexibly to disruptions.

### Job redesign and role overlap

- Some roles have been redesigned so employees perform a wider range of work.
- Examples mentioned include:
  - A caller also holding a security licence or supporting the fire command centre
  - A bellperson patrolling at the same time
  - A room attendant acting as a runner when staffing is insufficient
  - Back-of-house employees supporting breakfast during quieter periods
- Front-office work was traditionally specialised, but the hotel is moving toward employees being able to perform multiple duties.
- The hotel also employs or supports people with special needs.

### Common service errors

- Greeting guests was mentioned as a common area where mistakes occur.
- Product quality, service and employee attitude form part of the quality framework.
- Reputation performance is a core KPI for hotel leaders.

## Quality Standards and Audits

- Guest feedback is shared with department leaders manually.
- The hotel aims to respond to guests within 48 hours.
- A mystery audit is conducted approximately every six months.
- Corporate staff organise the audit.
- The audit score is a KPI, with a score of approximately 92 mentioned in the conversation.
- Corrective action must be completed within seven days.
- Minor physical details, such as a missing hanger or damaged hanger varnish, can affect quality compliance.
- Sofitel was described as having approximately 2,000 brand standards.
- Novotel was described as having approximately 200–300 brand standards.
