# PARKROYAL Visit — Main Points

## Pre-Check-In Process

- Before arrival, guests may receive an email inviting them to complete a pre-check-in form.
- Guests provide personal particulars and preferences so that the hotel can reserve a suitable room and expedite check-in.
- The submitted personal information may include a photograph or passport details.
- The hotel purges this information after seven days.

### Operational and privacy considerations

- Collecting information in advance reduces the amount of processing required at the front desk.
- The process involves sensitive identity information, making data access, retention and timely deletion important.
- Guest preferences can help with advance room allocation, but the hotel must balance personalisation with data-protection requirements.

## Arrival and Bell-Service Handover

- A bellperson asks arriving guests for their last name.
- The name is communicated to the front desk through an earpiece, allowing front-office staff to prepare for the guest’s arrival.
- During peak periods, the message may be missed.

### Problem identified

- The handover depends on verbal communication being heard and acted upon immediately.
- High noise levels, simultaneous requests or heavy arrival volume may cause guest information to be missed.
- A missed handover can reduce the intended benefit of recognising and preparing for the guest before they reach the counter.

## Self-Checkout and Queue Management

- Eligible guests can check out by scanning a QR code.
- Self-checkout is available only to guests who have no outstanding balance.
- A staff member positioned between the lifts and front desk directs each departing guest either to self-checkout or to the counter.
- This routing is intended to prevent unnecessary queues at the front desk.

### Problem identified

- Although the checkout itself is digital, the process still depends on an employee manually identifying and directing eligible guests.
- Incorrect or delayed routing may cause guests to join the wrong queue and increase congestion.
- The arrangement transfers part of the workload from the counter to a staff member managing guest flow in the lobby.

## Luggage Delivery

- The hotel plans to use robots to deliver luggage to guest rooms and reduce the workload of bell staff.
- The proposed process requires automatic doors and lift integration so that a robot can travel independently to guest floors.

### Operational issue being addressed

- Luggage delivery is labour-intensive and adds to bell-service workload, particularly during periods with many simultaneous arrivals.
- The proposed system also depends on reliable coordination among the robot, doors, lifts and room-access controls.

## VIP Recognition and Personalisation

- PARKROYAL is interested in using smart glasses to help employees recognise and serve VIP guests.
- The idea was discussed particularly in relation to the club lounge.
- The glasses could potentially help authorised employees identify a VIP and view relevant information such as:
  - Name
  - Allergies
  - Preferences
- The hotel showed greater interest in smart glasses because of concerns about protecting personal data and controlling who can access guest information.
- The current plan is to identify VIP guests when they tap their room card to enter the lounge.

### Problem identified

- Room-card recognition occurs only after the guest performs an action and may not support proactive recognition before entry.
- Employees must provide personalised service while avoiding unnecessary exposure of sensitive guest information.
- Any recognition system must define which employees can access guest identities, allergies and preferences, and under what circumstances.

## Emotional Intelligence in Guest Service

- PARKROYAL places strong emphasis on emotional intelligence when serving guests and carrying out upselling.
- Employees are expected to:
  - Observe their surroundings continuously
  - Recognise a guest’s emotional state
  - Anticipate when assistance may be needed
  - Adjust their communication and service approach accordingly
- Emotional awareness can also help employees identify appropriate opportunities for upselling without making the interaction feel forced.

### Problem identified

- After long working hours, employees may become tired or desensitised through repetition.
- Fatigue and familiarity with the role can reduce situational awareness and make subtle guest cues easier to overlook.
- The quality of emotionally attentive service may therefore be difficult to maintain consistently throughout a shift.

## Event Staffing and Service Consistency

- Event operations rely heavily on part-time employees.
- Part-time employees may have less familiarity with the hotel’s service expectations, procedures and guest-handling standards.

### Problem identified

- Service standards may be inconsistent when temporary staff have limited training or experience.
- Events create concentrated demand, so weaknesses in briefing, coordination or service execution can affect many guests at the same time.

## Main Problems Highlighted

1. Arrival information passed through an earpiece may be missed during peak periods.
2. Self-checkout still depends on manual guest routing to prevent front-desk congestion.
3. Bell staff face a high physical and operational workload when delivering luggage.
4. VIP personalisation must be balanced with PDPA and access-control concerns.
5. Long working hours and repetition may reduce employees’ emotional awareness and responsiveness.
6. Heavy reliance on part-time event staff may lead to inconsistent service standards.
