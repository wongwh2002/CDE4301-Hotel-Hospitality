# Interview-Note Formatting Guidelines

## Purpose

Use these guidelines to turn informal, live-typed interview notes into readable field notes without presenting them as a verbatim transcript.

The formatted notes should preserve what the interviewee communicated while clearly separating confirmed statements, uncertain shorthand and interviewer observations. Problem exploration should remain distinct from solution development.

## Source Description

At the start of each formatted document, state:

- Who was interviewed and their role
- The organisation or setting
- Whether the notes were typed live or reconstructed later
- Whether the notes are verbatim, paraphrased or mixed
- Whether observations and interviewer prompts are also present

Do not call the document a transcript unless exact speech was recorded.

## Formatting Rules

### Organisation

- Group notes by topic rather than preserving a confusing chronological order.
- Use descriptive headings and short bullet points.
- Keep relevant numbers, examples and operational details with their topic.
- Separate hotel context from problems and operational observations.
- Avoid repeating the same note under several headings unless the relationship is important.

### Wording

- Correct obvious spelling, grammar and hotel terminology.
- Expand shorthand only when its meaning is clear from context.
- Preserve the original meaning rather than making the writing sound more certain.
- Do not place reconstructed sentences inside quotation marks.
- Use neutral language such as “the manager stated,” “the notes indicate” or “was described as.”
- Avoid judgemental wording from rough notes. Reframe it as an expectation, communication or policy issue.

### Uncertainty

Use these markers when necessary:

| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[Unclear in raw notes]` | The intended meaning cannot be established safely |
| `[Verify]` | A number, system name or factual claim needs confirmation |
| `[Interviewer observation]` | The note records something directly noticed by the interviewer |
| `[Interviewer prompt]` | The note appears to be a question rather than the interviewee’s answer |

Do not guess the meaning of abbreviations, system names or incomplete fragments. Retain them in an “Unclear fragments” section for follow-up.

## Evidence and Attribution

- Treat live-typed notes as paraphrased field notes unless exact wording is known.
- Attribute general statements to the interviewee or notes without using quotation marks.
- Do not convert one person’s experience into an industry-wide conclusion.
- Mark property-specific statements separately from claims about the wider hotel industry.
- Keep interviewer observations distinct from what the interviewee reported.
- Retain the original raw notes in a separate file so later edits can be checked against the source.

## Problem-Focused Analysis

When a later analysis document is created, describe each problem using:

1. Problem statement
2. Context in which it occurs
3. People or departments affected
4. Contributing conditions
5. Operational or guest consequences
6. Supporting evidence from the notes
7. Missing information and follow-up questions

Do not treat a symptom, cause and consequence as if they were interchangeable.

## Treatment of Solutions

- Preserve solutions mentioned by the interviewee only when source completeness requires it.
- Do not develop, evaluate or recommend solutions in a problem-discovery document.
- Keep proposed technologies, automation and process changes separate from evidence describing the underlying problem.
- Do not assume that an existing or proposed system solves the problem simply because it was mentioned.

## Privacy and Sensitivity

- Do not include the interviewee’s name unless permission was given.
- Avoid publishing personal guest or employee information.
- Generalise nationalities or demographic examples when they are not necessary to understand the problem.
- Treat guest preferences, allergies, behaviour flags, payment arrangements and loyalty data as potentially sensitive.
- Avoid wording that stereotypes guests or employees based on nationality, age or background.

## Quality Check Before Publishing

- Does the document correctly describe itself as field notes rather than a transcript?
- Can every polished statement be traced to the raw notes?
- Are uncertain terms visibly marked rather than guessed?
- Are interviewer observations and prompts distinguishable from the manager’s statements?
- Have obvious spelling errors been corrected without changing meaning?
- Are sensitive or judgemental phrases rewritten neutrally?
- Are solutions kept separate from problem findings?
- Is the unedited raw-note file stored alongside the formatted version?
